# Research and design notes

Research consulted 17 November 2026. This is a speculative design; the 2045 and 2086 timeline, neighbors, institutions, and seeds are fictional.

## Online references

1. [Low-tech Magazine — About the Solar Powered Website](https://solar.lowtechmagazine.com/about/the-solar-website/)
   - Observed: the publication uses a lightweight static site, default typefaces, and an explicitly resource-conscious approach.
   - Design response: buildless HTML/CSS/JavaScript, system fonts, small original SVG illustrations, no third-party scripts, no autoplay media. This prototype does **not** claim to be solar-powered or to have measured its environmental impact.
2. [Library of Things — How Borrowing Works](https://www.libraryofthings.co.uk/how-borrowing-works)
   - Observed: neighborhood kiosks support borrowing objects, and the organization promotes practical skill sharing.
   - Design response: imagine physical community access points and everyday knowledge as a shared resource. The speculative garden is not affiliated with Library of Things and does not reproduce its service.

## Physical and spatial references

- Seed libraries: small contributions, descriptive labels, and the ability to carry something away informed the seed cards and personal pocket.
- Greenhouses: transparent enclosures, visible growth, and shared tending informed the orbital botanical illustration.
- Community noticeboards: equal prominence for offers and requests informed the garden's non-ranked presentation.

These are conceptual references, not claims of museum visits, fieldwork, or interviews. The SVG artwork is original and generated by the included Python source; no photographs or third-party visual assets are used.

## Critical decisions

- Separate fiction from functioning software: sample residents are labeled fictional; local storage is described before contribution.
- Avoid invented consent: no networking, accounts, messages, or automatic publication of visitor seeds.
- Design for agency: opt-in audio; visible motion control; system reduced-motion preference; remove saved seeds and contributions.
- Preserve access: semantic page landmarks, labeled controls, native dialogs, keyboard focus, text descriptions, responsive Flexbox layouts.
- Keep responsibility visible: the agreement explains imagined governance and recognizes moderation, language access, and participatory testing as future work.
- Limitations: English-only text; no user research or assistive-technology user testing; storage does not synchronize between devices or origins. Real community deployment would need substantially more work.

## Process

Brief → future scenario → online precedents → botanical visual language → three-page journey → local interactions → desktop/mobile browser checks → reflection and submission package.
