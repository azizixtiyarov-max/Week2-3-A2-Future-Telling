# Explain Common Ground in one minute

“My project is called Common Ground. It imagines the internet in 2086 as a living community garden. People explore digital seeds containing knowledge, memories, or care.

“The first page introduces the world. The second lets visitors explore seeds, save them in a personal pocket, and plant their own contribution. The third explains the community’s values: consent, accessibility, and shared ownership.

“I want this future to empower people whose contributions are easily overlooked online, including quiet participants, older neighbors, newcomers, and people with everyday skills. Repair knowledge or an offer of company can be valuable without becoming popular.

“Glowing flowers and an orbital greenhouse combine nature with futuristic technology. The dark background feels quiet; lavender and green suggest imagination and growth. CSS Flexbox makes the layouts adapt to different screen sizes.

“The project asks: what would the internet look like if we designed it around belonging and mutual care?”

## A quick live demonstration

1. Start at `dist/index.html`: explain the 2086 setting and botanical network.
2. Click **Enter the garden**, then **Knowledge**: show how a visitor chooses a topic.
3. Open **A second life for small things**: describe a seed as a contribution with a story and a small action.
4. Click **Keep in my pocket**, close the seed, and choose **My pocket**.
5. Choose **Plant a seed** near the bottom and add an example contribution.
6. Open **Our agreement**: explain that governance and inclusion are part of design.

## Likely questions

**What is futuristic?**
The imagined web connects physical greenhouses and digital spaces through a community-owned planetary network. The orbital botanical interface turns connection into something people tend together.

**Who is empowered?**
People with ordinary knowledge, people starting again in a community, and those who prefer quieter forms of participation.

**Why plants?**
A seed suggests potential. Growth takes time and care; many different contributions can coexist. It makes an abstract network feel tangible.

**How do the pages connect?**
The journey is: imagine the future → experience it → understand its responsibilities. Shared navigation, colors, typography, and botanical imagery keep it coherent.

**Where is Flexbox used?**
In the header and navigation, hero section, three pathways, seed filters, wrapping seed-card layout, planting form, timeline, and footer. Media queries change these arrangements on narrow screens.

**What actually works?**
Navigation, category filters, seed dialogs, saved favorites, local seed creation/removal, expandable principles, motion controls, and optional synthesized audio.

**Is it a real social network?**
No. The founding residents and timeline are fictional. Personal seeds and favorites stay in the visitor’s browser using localStorage. There is no shared database or messaging service.

**What is the designer’s responsibility?**
Consider consent, access, power, maintenance, and exclusion alongside appearance. This prototype makes some commitments visible, but real community development would require research with participants and accountable stewardship.

**What inspired it?**
Low-tech Magazine’s resource-conscious website, Library of Things’ local sharing model, seed libraries, and greenhouse architecture. See `RESEARCH.md` for sources.

## Explain the revision

“The critique showed me that visitors understood the garden’s atmosphere before they understood its purpose. I made the purpose explicit: plant a thought, add chapters, and watch its story grow. A guided example shows how an old market memory becomes a new friendship. The flowers now have distinct stages and more vivid colors. This makes growth something visitors can actually do.”

For the revised demo, choose **Plant my first seed**, try a writing prompt, and submit. Under **My seeds**, open your contribution and add a chapter: it becomes a bud. Add another: it blooms. Show that the original writing and both chapters remain together in the story history.
